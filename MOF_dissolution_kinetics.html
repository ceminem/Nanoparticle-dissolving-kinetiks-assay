    How k is calculated
    1. For a trial value of k, the model y = y∞ (1 − e−kt) is linear in y∞, so the best plateau has an exact solution: y∞ = Σ giyi / Σ gi², with gi = 1 − e−k ti. With a free start value, y₀ and y∞ come from ordinary linear regression of y on g.
    2. The sum of squared residuals is computed for 600 values of k, spaced evenly on a log scale from 0.01 / tmax to 30 / tmin.
    3. The best k from that scan is refined by golden-section search between its two neighbours.
    4. The SE of k comes from the curvature of the fit: s² (JᵀJ)⁻¹, with J the derivatives of the model with respect to each parameter and s² = residual sum of squares / (points − parameters).
    5. t½ = ln 2 / k. All points are weighted equally; the t = 0 point is not added as data, the model itself passes through zero.
